
# EU Checkout Rules for Small Stores: Withdrawal Button and Guarantee Notice

Two items in an EU checkout generate most of the warning letters we see: a missing withdrawal function and a missing guarantee notice. Both are cheap to fix and expensive to ignore.

This is an operational checklist, not legal advice. It is what we check before a store takes money.

## 1. The withdrawal function

Since mid-2026, German courts have treated a checkout without a functioning withdrawal path as a competition violation. Letters arrive with a €390 plus VAT cost claim attached.

What "functioning" means in practice:

- The buyer can reach the withdrawal action **from the checkout page**, not from an email burying a link.
- The flow works without login and without contacting support.
- A confirmation is shown and stored (email is fine).
- The button text is unambiguous: withdraw / cancel the contract.

**Test:** place a test order, then try to withdraw as a customer would, on a phone, without help.

## 2. The guarantee notice

Separate from the statutory warranty, the EU sale-of-goods rules require a notice about the legal guarantee where the consumer buys. Since 27 September 2026 that notice has to appear at the point of sale, not only in the terms.

Minimum content: that a legal guarantee of conformity applies, its duration in your market, and where the details are.

**Test:** open your checkout on a phone and search the visible text for "guarantee" / "Gewährleistung". If it is only in the terms page, it is not at the point of sale.

## 3. The other ten things we check

1. Total price including tax and shipping before the final button.
2. Delivery time or date, stated before purchase.
3. Trader identity and address, reachable.
4. Withdrawal policy linked at checkout, not only in the footer.
5. Order confirmation on a durable medium (email).
6. Price reduction claims backed by a prior price.
7. Consent for any pre-ticked extra, clearly not pre-ticked.
8. Payment method fees, if any, disclosed.
9. Country-specific consumer information for each market you ship to.
10. Cookie/consent behaviour matching what your pages actually load.

## FAQ

### Is the withdrawal button required in every EU country?
The German enforcement is the loudest, and the EU-wide consumer rules push the same direction. If you sell to Germany, treat it as required.

### Can I put the guarantee notice in the terms only?
No. The requirement is a notice at the point of sale; the terms page is where the details live.
