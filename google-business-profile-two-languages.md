
# Running a Google Business Profile in Two Languages (Without Duplicating It)

The most common mistake is creating a second profile for the second language. Google treats that as a duplicate, and the duplicate is the one that gets suspended.

One profile can serve two languages. Eight settings matter.

## 1. Name: do not translate it
The business name is a brand, not a description. Translating it creates a mismatch with signage, invoices and reviews.

**Exception:** if the business legally trades under both names and the signage shows both, mirror the signage exactly.

## 2. Category: pick the primary in the market's language
Primary category drives which queries you appear for. Choose the category that matches how local customers search, not a literal translation of your English category.

## 3. Description: two versions, one field
Google gives you one description field. The practical approach: lead with the local language (the one most searchers use), then one short paragraph in the second language, clearly separated.

Do not stuff keywords in either language. Descriptions are read by humans deciding whether to call.

## 4. Services and products: separate items, both languages
These are list items, so you can have both languages without conflict. Name each item in the language the customer would say it in.

## 5. Posts: alternate, do not duplicate
Two posts saying the same thing in two languages on the same day look like spam. Alternate: one post this week in language A, next week in language B, each with its own photo.

## 6. Q&A: seed the questions you actually get
Post the three questions you answer on the phone every week, in both languages, and answer them. This is the only place where you can put words in a customer's mouth without it being deceptive — you are answering your own FAQ.

## 7. Reviews: reply in the language of the review
A five-star review in Chinese answered in English signals "nobody here speaks Chinese". Reply in the review's language, even briefly.

## 8. Website link: one landing page per language
Point the profile to the language the majority uses, and make sure the other language is one click away on that page. Do not rotate the link weekly; it resets signals.

## What breaks when you rush it

- A second profile "for the English customers" → duplicate suspension.
- Machine-translated category → you appear for the wrong intent.
- Description in two languages with a slash → neither reads well.
- Posts duplicated on the same day → suppressed as low quality.
- Review replies in the wrong language → trust drops even when the rating is high.

## The 30-minute pass

1. Confirm one profile only.
2. Set primary category in the local search language.
3. Rewrite description: local language first, second language in one short block.
4. Add services in both languages.
5. Post this week in the language you have been ignoring.
6. Answer three real questions in both languages.
7. Reply to the last ten reviews in their own language.
8. Check the website link lands on the right language page.
